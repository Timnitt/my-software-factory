How the Greeks Built: Research Findings on Ancient Greek Architectural Processes

This page gathers research findings on how ancient Greek buildings, mainly temples, were designed, constructed, and made from materials. Each section opens with a short synthesis, followed by paper cards with a confidence badge showing how well each source could be checked.

Note: the summaries below came from abstracts, search results and press coverage, not from full texts.

1. Design

Synthesis

  • Temples were laid out from a small module (triglyph width or column diameter) plus whole-number ratios (Wilson Jones), though how strict this was is debated.
  • Design used a fixed foot (about 327 mm proposed).
  • At Didyma, plans, elevations and details were incised 1:1 on walls as working templates.
  • Refinements like entasis were planned in advance, but their purpose is contested.
  • Planning could fail (the unfinished Didyma column).
Verified abstract

Towards Understanding Greek Temple Design: General Considerations

J. J. Coulton · 1975 · Annual of the British School at Athens 70, pp. 59-99

Sets out technical constraints on ancient architects and tests the proportional/modular theories usually taken for granted, proposing more objective tests. (Abstract only.)

https://doi.org/10.1017/S0068245400006535

Verified abstract

Doric Measure and Architectural Design 2: A Modular Reading of the Classical Temple

Mark Wilson Jones · 2001 · American Journal of Archaeology 105(4), 675-713

Compares a group of Doric temples and argues they were laid out by proportional rules plus a module with triglyph width as the key unit. Proposes a foot of about 327 mm.

https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/doric-measure-and-architectural-design-2-a-modular-reading-of-the/

Verified abstract

Architekturforschungen in Didyma II: Die unfertig stehende Säule des Apollontempels: ein Tiefpunkt antiker Säulenplanung

L. Haselberger, E. Koenigsaecker, S. Prignitz · 2023 · Archäologischer Anzeiger 1, pp. 1-110

New measurements of the unfinished Didyma column show it could not have been finished even with a straight shaft, let alone the entasis in the incised design drawing. Read as a planning failure that wasted about a year of the building budget.

https://publications.dainst.org/journals/aa/article/view/4126

PreprintContested

The illusion of illusions: There are no optical corrections in the Parthenon

Alain Goriely · Oct 2025 · arXiv:2510.16831 preprint (journal publication not confirmed)

Traces the optical-correction explanation back to Vitruvius and its 19th-century revival. Argues the assumed illusions are nonexistent or too small, and suggests practical craft reasons instead.

https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16831v1

Press or snippet only (lower confidence)

The Construction Plans for the Temple of Apollo at Didyma

Lothar Haselberger · Dec 1985 · Scientific American 253(6), p. 126 · library catalog record

Incised 1:1 drawings found on cella walls in 1979 show plans, elevations and details, including entasis.

https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edb,10558707

2. Construction

Synthesis

  • Lifting likely used rope-and-frame or pulley-style devices from the late 7th c. BC; lewis/tong cuttings are well attested by about 515 BC.
  • Compound-pulley theory appears in texts only in the 3rd c. BC; earlier machines are debated (Coulton vs Pierattini).
  • Building ran through public accounts: boards of overseers inventoried unfinished blocks, and contractors were paid by piece.
  • Labour was mixed: citizens, metics and slaves (slaves' wages paid via masters).
  • Temple building was an economic and prestige undertaking.
  • Quarrying was organised: different Parthenon elements came from different pits.
Verified abstract

Lifting in Early Greek Architecture

J. J. Coulton · 1974 · Journal of Hellenic Studies 94, 1-19

Questions the assumption that Archaic builders used Vitruvian compound-pulley cranes. Simple pulleys were known in the Near East by the 9th c. BC; the earliest clear compound-pulley evidence is pseudo-Aristotle's Mechanical Problems (about early 3rd c. BC). (Abstract only.)

https://doi.org/10.2307/630416

Press coverage onlyContested

Interpreting rope channels: lifting, setting and the birth of Greek monumental architecture

Alessandro Pierattini · c. 2019 · Annual of the British School at Athens

Early ashlar blocks from Isthmia and Corinth (c. 700-650 BC, about 200-400 kg) have paired underside rope grooves. Read as evidence of lifting and setting with a rope-and-frame device, about 150 years before lewis-hole evidence.

https://doi.org/10.1017/S0068245419000030 · Press: https://www.cambridge.org/core/blog/?p=31049

Press or snippet only

The Economics of Greek Temple Building

A. M. Burford · 1965 · Proceedings of the Cambridge Philological Society 11, 21-34

Treats temple building as a major factor in Greek city economics: a costly public undertaking and prestige display. Covers public finance, labour, contracting, and boom-and-bust. (Page extract only.)

https://doi.org/10.1017/S0068673500003175

Press or snippet only

The Erechtheion Construction Work Inventory (IG I³ 474) and the Dörpfeld Temple

Jari Pakkanen · 2006 · American Journal of Archaeology 110(2), 275-281

The 409/8 BC inventory lists half-finished blocks with dimensions when a new board took over. Argues the blocks belong to the Erechtheion itself, not evidence of Old Athena Temple demolition, and also estimates the Doric foot. (Partly from search summaries.)

https://ajaonline.org/note/141/

Press or snippet only

The Last Erechtheion Building Accounts

S. D. Lambert · Attic Inscriptions Online Paper 7 (date uncertain, about 2016)

Covers IG I³ 474-475 and 476 plus two Ionic-script accounts. Related: the Acropolis Museum page says the accounts record contractors, builders and labourers, with wages for column fluting often paid to slaves via masters.

https://atticinscriptions.com/papers/aio-papers-7 · Related: https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en/financial-accounts-construction-erechtheion

3. Materials & Structure

Synthesis

  • Isotope provenance is not a simple fingerprint: Pentelic marble varies within the mountain, so dense quarry databases are needed. Naxian work found new outcrops and suggested a statue from several quarries.
  • Mortarless drum columns are not weak: sliding and rocking dissipate energy; the response is nonlinear and sensitive.
  • Connectors are a trade-off: clamps raise displacement capacity, but rigid retrofit (titanium dowels) may remove energy-absorbing slip; corroded early-20th-c. clamps damaged marble.
  • Mortar and polychromy evidence comes mostly from Bronze Age/Hellenistic contexts and Parthenon pigments; timber-roof evidence is mostly reconstruction.
Press or snippet only

Characterization of Intra-Quarry Variability in Pentelic Marble Using Stable Isotopes: A Case Study of the Parthenon

Scott Pike · 2025 · Minerals 15(3), 231

610 samples from 83 quarry pits on Mt Pentelikon; isotope values differ significantly between regions. Parthenon West Pediment marble is tied to upslope Aspra Marmara quarries, structural blocks to lower Spilia Divail and other pits. Caveat: sourcing claims not independently replicated. (Details from search snippets.)

https://doi.org/10.3390/min15030231

News item, not primary paper

Search for Sculptor's Stone Reveals New Marble Outcrops

Rachel Berkowitz · 2021 · Physics Magazine 14, 166

News item on Pike & Sheedy work: isotopes from a Colossus of the Naxians fragment on Delos matched newly found outcrops in Sangri Valley, Naxos. The statue was likely made from several Naxian quarries.

https://physics.aps.org/articles/v14/166

Verified abstract

Columns of Greek Temples under seismic loading

I. Vayas, M.-E. Dasiou, A. Marinelli · 2007 · Bautechnik 84(6), 388-396

In mortarless stacked drums, sliding and rocking act as energy absorption. Such columns may be less vulnerable than modern structures.

https://doi.org/10.1002/bate.200710034

Press or snippet only

Parthenon façade colonnade pushover analysis (exact title not seen)

Diaferio, Foti, Vacca · 2015 · EUROMECH Colloquium 562

A UDEC 2D model with rigid drums: three connection levels reach the same collapse load, but displacement capacity rises as connectors are added.

https://iris.poliba.it/handle/11589/23366

Verified abstract

Mortars from the Palace of Knossos in Crete, Greece: A Multi-Analytical Approach

Carvalho et al. · 2022 · Minerals 12(1), 30

Microscopy, XRD, FTIR and TG/DTA show mostly lime-based mortars (aerial and hydraulic), some gypsum-based; composition varies between areas. (Bronze Age, not classical.)

https://doi.org/10.3390/min12010030

Press coverage only

Parthenon sculpture polychromy (exact title/DOI not seen)

Giovanni Verri and team · 2023 · Antiquity

Visible-induced luminescence imaging mapped Egyptian blue on pedimental sculptures; traces of white and purple.

https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/scientific-analysis-reveals-the-true-colours-of-the-parthenon-sculptures
